# File Directory: papers/P031.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Mst. Nilufar Yasmin, Mohammad Mizanur Rahman, Nusrat Y. Ahmed, Amrin Binte Ahmed, Adisha Dulmini, Muhammad Khairul Alam, Atikur Rahman, Rumana Rois (2026)
* **Publication Venue:** Scientific Reports (Nature Portfolio)
* **Target Population & Region:** University students | Bangladesh
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 424

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To apply an exploratory, explainable machine learning framework to analyze and predict complex patterns of depression, anxiety, and stress among university students in Bangladesh during a period of significant social unrest.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Depression, Anxiety, and Stress Scale (DASS-21) and the Internet Addiction Test (IAT).

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** Psychological outcomes (DASS-21), Internet Addiction (IAT), demographic data (gender, university type, academic discipline, BMI), and behavioral/psychosocial variables (e.g., waking up from bad dreams, memory loss symptoms, misbehavior, smoking status, religious activity, Google Classroom usage).

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** Decision Trees (DT), Random Forests (RF), Support Vector Machines (SVM), Logistic Regression (LR), Adaptive Boosting (AdaBoost), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM), and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN).
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** The overall predictive performance was relatively modest due to the highly skewed, high-symptom sample. Support Vector Machine (SVM) demonstrated the most stable performance for predicting Depression (Accuracy = 0.696, F1 = 0.787) and Stress (Accuracy = 0.660, F1 = 0.688). XGBoost was selected for Anxiety (Accuracy = 0.715, F1 = 0.819) due to a better balance of sensitivity and specificity.
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** Highly rigorous use of XAI. Utilized the Boruta algorithm for feature selection, followed by SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) for global feature importance and LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations) for local, individual-level prediction transparency. Sankey diagrams were also used to visualize feature-outcome associations.

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The authors explicitly state that their explainable ML framework is an exploratory, hypothesis-generating tool for pattern recognition. Because the models largely leveraged overlapping symptomatology (e.g., using "bad dreams" to predict "depression") rather than independent antecedent risk factors, they strongly advise against deploying this specific model as a clinical screening tool. 

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Addresses a massive geographical gap by conducting an advanced ML/XAI study specifically in Bangladesh (an LMIC); utilizes state-of-the-art explainability techniques (SHAP and LIME) to demystify black-box ML models; demonstrates acute awareness of the tautological risks in using self-reported symptoms to predict other self-reported symptoms.
* **Key Limitations:** Non-probability purposive online sampling during a period of extreme national political unrest (July 2024 student protests) resulted in a highly skewed sample with exceptionally high distress prevalence (e.g., 73.4% severe anxiety), limiting generalizability; cross-sectional design prevents causal inference.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Complete absence of objective behavioral data or passive digital logging. Despite assessing variables like Internet Addiction, sleep quality, and BMI, all data was entirely self-reported. The authors explicitly call for future research to utilize longitudinal tracking to establish temporal inference, which passive sensing could facilitate.
