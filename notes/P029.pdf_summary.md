## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Rasikh Tariq, M. G. Orozco-del-Castillo, Muhammad Tayyab Zamir, Maria Soledad Ramírez-Montoya, Tabbi Wilberforce (2025)
* **Publication Venue:** Scientific Reports (Nature Portfolio)
* **Target Population & Region:** University students | Mexico
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 1,100

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To develop a cost-effective, survey-based machine learning classification model for student stress detection, utilizing eXplainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) to ensure transparent and actionable institutional decision-making.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** A custom survey predicting discrete stress levels (0 = low, 1 = medium, 2 = high).

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** 21 features spanning five domains: Psychological (anxiety level, self-esteem, mental health history, depression), Physiological (headache, blood pressure, sleep quality, breathing problems), Social (peer pressure, teacher-student relationship, extracurricular activities, social support, bullying), Environmental (noise level, living conditions, safety, basic needs), and Academic (academic performance, study load, future career concerns).

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** Logistic Regression, Support Vector Machine (SVM), Decision Tree, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, and XGBoost.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** Random Forest achieved the most balanced high performance with an Accuracy of 0.88, Precision of 0.88, Recall of 0.88, and F1-Score of 0.88. Logistic Regression and SVM also performed comparably well (Accuracy = 0.89).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) and traditional Feature Importance plots were heavily utilized to unpack the "black-box" models. SHAP analysis revealed that self-reported *blood pressure*, *perceived safety*, *sleep quality*, *teacher-student relationships*, and *extracurricular activities* were the top predictors of stress.

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The study recommends using the XAI insights to drive institutional policy changes, specifically: implementing campus health monitoring (focusing on sleep hygiene and blood pressure), improving campus safety protocols, funding extracurricular clubs to build social networks, and training faculty in relational pedagogy to improve teacher-student dynamics.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Superb use of modern Explainable AI (SHAP) to interpret ML models, allowing for targeted policy recommendations; utilizes a highly multidimensional dataset (21 features across 5 life domains).
* **Key Limitations:** **The article has been formally retracted.** Additionally, it relies on self-reported physiological markers (e.g., asking students to self-report their blood pressure), which lacks clinical validity compared to actual sensor data; cross-sectional design prevents causal tracking.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Despite its data-driven approach, it completely lacks objective, passive digital logging (e.g., smartphone usage or wearable sensor data), relying entirely on subjective self-report questionnaires. The study highlights the need for longitudinal tracking to understand the temporal dynamics of stress.
