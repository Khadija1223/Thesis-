# File Directory: papers/P026.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Nils Olson, Renate Oberhoffer-Fritz, Barbara Reiner, Thorsten Schulz (2025)
* **Publication Venue:** BMC Psychology
* **Target Population & Region:** University students across five academic disciplines (Informatics, Mechanical Engineering, Sports/Health Sciences, Medicine, Economic Sciences) | Bavaria, Germany (Technical University of Munich)
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 947

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To investigate and compare the prevalence of stress, burnout, and study engagement across different academic majors, specifically challenging the prevalent assumption that medical students are the most vulnerable group.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Maslach Burnout Inventory Short Form for Students (MBI-SS). The study also utilized the Utrecht Work Engagement Scale for Students (UWES-S 9) and the Perceived Stress Scale (PSS-10).

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** Sociodemographic factors (age, gender), academic faculty affiliation, number of semesters studied, self-reported academic workload (weekly hours), occupational working hours (side-jobs), and study satisfaction.

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None. The study employed traditional inferential statistics, specifically Analysis of Variance (ANOVA/Welch-ANOVA), pairwise comparisons (Tukey's test), and binary logistic regression using SPSS.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** The binary logistic regression identified significant predictors for burnout dimensions. For example, higher study satisfaction significantly reduced the odds of emotional exhaustion (OR = 0.702), while increased academic workload slightly increased the odds of cynicism (OR = 1.013) but also increased study engagement (OR = 1.014).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable. (Interpretability relies on standard white-box logistic regression Odds Ratios).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The study recommends institutional interventions: moving away from a "one-size-fits-all" approach to mental health, implementing tailored stress management courses specific to different faculties (especially for Informatics and Engineering students who showed the highest burnout), and focusing on fostering overall study satisfaction rather than merely attempting to eliminate academic stressors.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Successfully broadens the scope of student burnout research beyond the medical/nursing fields, providing a direct, empirical comparison across multiple disciplines; highlights the often-overlooked high burnout rates among Informatics and Engineering students; examines both negative (burnout) and positive (engagement) academic paradigms.
* **Key Limitations:** Cross-sectional design prevents the establishment of temporal or causal relationships; the very low response rate (~10%) raises concerns about self-selection bias; relies entirely on subclinical self-assessments rather than clinical diagnoses.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Complete absence of objective behavioral data, such as passive digital logging, to verify self-reported metrics like weekly study hours or sleep; lack of predictive Machine Learning (ML) models or Explainable AI (XAI) frameworks; geographically restricted to a single high-income European country (Germany), leaving gaps regarding LMICs such as Bangladesh.
