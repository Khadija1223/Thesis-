# File Directory: papers/P019.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Seung-Yi Choi, Jung-Hee Kim (2024)
* **Publication Venue:** Frontiers in Public Health
* **Target Population & Region:** Generation Z university students | Republic of Korea
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 330

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To culturally adapt, investigate the factor structure, and verify the validity and reliability of the Korean version of the Digital Burnout Scale (DBS) among Generation Z university students.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Digital Burnout Scale (DBS) (Assessing three dimensions: digital aging, digital deprivation, and emotional exhaustion). The Maslach Burnout Inventory-Student Survey (MBI-SS) and the Digital Addiction Scale (K-DAS) were used to test concurrent validity.

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** Self-reported daily digital device use time, primary purpose of digital device use, digital burnout (DBS), digital addiction (K-DAS), academic burnout (MBI-SS), and demographic factors (age, gender, academic grade, major, residence).

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None. The study utilized traditional psychometric validation methods, primarily Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) using AMOS 18 and Pearson correlation analysis using SPSS 22.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** The 3-factor Confirmatory Factor Analysis model for the 24-item Korean DBS demonstrated an adequate fit to the data (CFI = 0.91, TLI = 0.90, RMSEA = 0.08, SRMR = 0.07).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable. (Interpretability relies on standard white-box psychometric factor loadings and construct validity metrics).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The study recommends educational interventions: developing healthy digital usage education programs, encouraging regular breaks and limiting screen time before sleeping, enhancing digital control through time management education, and promoting offline leisure activities (e.g., meditation and yoga) to alleviate digital-induced emotional exhaustion.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Validates a highly relevant, contemporary tool (DBS) that captures unique modern stressors ("digital aging" and "digital deprivation") overlooked by traditional academic burnout scales; specifically targets the highly digitized Generation Z demographic.
* **Key Limitations:** Cross-sectional design prevents establishing causal relationships between digital usage and burnout; online data collection may exclude individuals with low internet accessibility; focused solely on university students, limiting generalizability to other populations like working professionals or older adults.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Complete reliance on self-report methods for tracking digital behavior (e.g., self-reported screen time) which is prone to estimation errors; the authors explicitly note the need for future research to incorporate objective data collection (such as passive digital logging or physiological sensors); absence of predictive Machine Learning models or Explainable AI; lack of cross-cultural validation in LMICs (e.g., Bangladesh).
