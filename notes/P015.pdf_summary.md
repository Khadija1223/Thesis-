# File Directory: papers/P015.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Reda Ebrahim Mohamed Elashram, Liyla Marzouk Alamri (2026)
* **Publication Venue:** Frontiers in Psychology
* **Target Population & Region:** Graduate students (Master's and Ph.D.) in special education departments | Saudi Arabia (6 public universities)
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 367

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To investigate the relationships between AI application usage patterns, academic resilience, and burnout among graduate special education students, and to determine the predictive capacity of AI use and resilience on burnout.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Maslach Burnout Inventory-Student Survey (MBI-SS) (Abbreviated 10-item version adapted for the Saudi context). The Brief Resilience Scale (BRS) was also used.

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** AI application usage patterns (a newly developed 20-item scale covering Cognitive/Technical Proficiency, Academic Application in Research/Writing, and Specialization/Adaptation), Academic Resilience (BRS), and Burnout (MBI-SS). Demographic factors included gender, academic level, and specialization track.

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None. The study relied exclusively on traditional statistical methods, specifically standard multiple regression, simple linear regression, and Pearson correlations using SPSS.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** A standard multiple regression model demonstrated that AI application usage patterns explained 34.1% of the variance in burnout (R² = 0.341), while academic resilience explained 19.1% (R² = 0.191).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable. (Interpretability relies on standard white-box regression coefficients).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were generated.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The study provides institutional policy recommendations, suggesting that universities implement structured AI training programs, institutional resilience interventions, and clear policies to regulate AI use to preserve academic integrity while optimizing research workloads.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Focuses on a highly specific and under-researched demographic (graduate special education students instead of the usual undergraduate medical students); challenges the assumption that AI use strictly exacerbates burnout, demonstrating it can act as a protective "resource" within the Job Demands-Resources (JD-R) model; develops a culturally tailored scale for AI usage.
* **Key Limitations:** Cross-sectional, correlational design restricts the ability to infer direct causality; convenience sampling limits broad generalizability; reliance on self-report instruments exposes findings to socially desirable response biases.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Complete absence of objective behavioral data, such as passive digital logging of actual AI usage to corroborate self-reports; absence of predictive Machine Learning models or Explainable AI frameworks; lack of longitudinal studies; while addressing the Saudi context, it lacks comparisons with lower-middle-income countries (LMICs) such as Bangladesh.
