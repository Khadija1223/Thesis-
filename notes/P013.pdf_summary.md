# File Directory: papers/P013.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Wasit Wongtrakul, Yodying Dangprapai, Nattha Saisavoey, Naratip Sa-nguanpanich (2023)
* **Publication Venue:** Frontiers in Psychology
* **Target Population & Region:** Preclinical medical students | Bangkok, Thailand
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 426

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To culturally adapt, translate, and validate the Maslach Burnout Inventory-Student Survey (MBI-SS) for use in a Thai context among preclinical medical students.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Maslach Burnout Inventory-Student Survey (MBI-SS) (16 items assessing emotional exhaustion, cynicism, and academic efficacy).

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** The translated 16-item Thai MBI-SS scale and basic demographic data (class year, gender, age, hometown region).

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None. The study relied exclusively on traditional psychometric and statistical methods, specifically Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA), Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA), and McDonald's omega coefficients.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** The 3-factor Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) model demonstrated acceptable goodness-of-fit indices: RMSEA = 0.062, SRMR = 0.055, CFI = 0.964, and TLI = 0.957.
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable. (Interpretability relies on standard white-box factor loadings).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The study's outcome is the validated questionnaire itself, which the authors recommend using for early identification of burnout to facilitate campus-based health promotion programs (e.g., mindfulness, physical activity, and self-compassion training).

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Successfully adapts and validates the gold-standard burnout assessment tool (MBI-SS) for a highly stressed, under-researched demographic in Thailand; utilizes rigorous statistical validation (EFA, CFA, and test-retest reliability).
* **Key Limitations:** The study was limited to preclinical medical students from a single university in Thailand, limiting generalizability to clinical students, other academic disciplines, or other institutions; low test-retest response rate (43.9%); lack of external validation.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Complete absence of objective behavioral data, such as passive digital logging or physiological sensor data, relying entirely on self-reports; lack of predictive Machine Learning (ML) models to forecast burnout; absence of Explainable AI (XAI) frameworks for intervention design; while it addresses Thailand, it lacks cross-cultural comparisons with other LMIC regions (e.g., Bangladesh).
