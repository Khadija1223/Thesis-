# File Directory: papers/P014.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Yuanyuan Chen, Mengyun Wang, Shujuan Yuan, Yan Zhao (2025)
* **Publication Venue:** Frontiers in Psychology
* **Target Population & Region:** College students | China (Multiple cities: Shanghai, Guangzhou, Xi'an, Haikou, Hefei)
* **Sample Size ($N$):** Multiple studies: Study 1 (N=547), Study 2 (N=687), Study 3 (N=1081 for status survey, N=892 for correlation).

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To develop and psychometrically validate a new instrument, the Conversational AI Dependence Scale (CAIDS), and to explore its relationship with psychological adaptation, stress, and subjective wellbeing among Chinese college students.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** The newly developed CAIDS (20-item scale). It was validated alongside the Depression-Anxiety-Stress Scale (DASS-21), Athens Insomnia Scale, and Subjective Wellbeing scales.

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** CAIDS dimensions (uncontrollability, withdrawal symptoms, mood modification, negative impact), sleep problems, functional difficulties (friendship, family, academic), depression, anxiety, stress (DASS-21), subjective wellbeing, social media addiction, loneliness, and insecure attachment. Demographic variables included gender, age, grade, income, and region.

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None. The study relied on traditional psychometric and statistical methods, specifically Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA), Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) using Mplus, ANOVA, and regression analyses using SPSS.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** The 4-factor CFA model for the CAIDS demonstrated good construct validity and fit indices (RMSEA = 0.07, SRMR = 0.06, CFI = 0.92, TLI = 0.91).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable. (Interpretability relies on standard psychometric factor loadings and regression coefficients).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The study recommends institutional and curricular interventions, proposing that universities offer courses titled "Digital Well-being and Emotional Regulation" that integrate mindfulness training to manage AI use, and establish a comprehensive "Human-centered AI Literacy" training system.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Addresses a highly contemporary issue by developing a specific psychometric tool (CAIDS) for Conversational AI dependency, explicitly distinguishing it from general problematic internet use; utilizes a robust mixed-methods approach combining qualitative interviews with multi-stage quantitative validation.
* **Key Limitations:** The cross-sectional design restricts the ability to draw causal inferences between AI dependence and negative psychological outcomes; the sample consists exclusively of Chinese college students, limiting broad cross-cultural generalizability.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Complete absence of objective behavioral data, such as passive digital logging of actual AI tool usage (e.g., chat logs, screen time, prompt frequency) to corroborate self-reports; absence of predictive Machine Learning models or Explainable AI frameworks; lack of validation in non-Chinese contexts, specifically other LMICs like Bangladesh.
