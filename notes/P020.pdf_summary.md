# File Directory: papers/P020.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Yunfei Tao, Zhaozhi Liu, Li Huang, Haowei Liu, Haodong Tian, Jinlong Wu, Lan Li, Li Peng (2024)
* **Publication Venue:** Frontiers in Public Health
* **Target Population & Region:** College/University students | Chongqing, China
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 23,652

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To explore the relationship between smartphone dependence and sleep quality among college students, and to examine the chain-mediating roles of negative emotions and health-promoting behaviors in this relationship.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Depression Anxiety Stress Scales (DASS-21) to assess negative emotions.

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** Smartphone dependence (Mobile Phone Involvement Questionnaire - MPIQ), sleep quality (Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index - PSQI), negative emotions (DASS-21), and health-promoting behaviors (Health-Promoting Lifestyle Profile II - HPLP-II). Demographic factors included gender, place of residence, parents' education, only child status, smoking, and alcohol consumption.

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None. The study relied on traditional statistical modeling, utilizing hierarchical linear regression and chain mediation analysis via the SPSS PROCESS macro.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** The chain mediation model demonstrated that smartphone dependence significantly and positively predicts poor sleep quality (total effect = 0.304). The indirect effects through negative emotions and health-promoting behaviors accounted for 56.06% of this total effect.
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable. (Interpretability relies on standard white-box regression coefficients and mediation effect sizes).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The study recommends public health and educational interventions focused on increasing health-promoting behaviors (e.g., outdoor activities, physical exercise, proper nutrition) and providing psychological support to alleviate negative emotions, which in turn can break the cycle of smartphone dependence and poor sleep.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Exceptionally large sample size (N=23,652) ensuring high statistical power; successfully integrates behavioral (lifestyle) and affective (emotions) dimensions into a complex chain-mediation model to explain the impacts of smartphone dependence.
* **Key Limitations:** Cross-sectional design prevents the establishment of definitive causal relationships; the study assumes smartphone use is purely detrimental, ignoring the potential benefits of digital tools (e.g., mHealth apps); demographic factors were used as covariates rather than examined as moderators of the mediation effects.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Complete absence of objective behavioral data, such as passive digital logging (e.g., actual screen time records) and objective physiological sleep tracking (e.g., actigraphy), relying entirely on subjective self-reports; absence of predictive Machine Learning (ML) models and Explainable AI (XAI); lacks cross-cultural validation in LMIC contexts outside of China (e.g., Bangladesh).
