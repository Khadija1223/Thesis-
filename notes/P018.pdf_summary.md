# File Directory: papers/P018.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Nils Olson, Renate Oberhoffer-Fritz, Barbara Reiner, Thorsten Schulz (2023)
* **Publication Venue:** Frontiers in Public Health
* **Target Population & Region:** University students (all academic subjects) | Bavaria, Germany
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 3,451

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To identify socio-demographic and study-related factors contributing to both student burnout and study engagement across a diverse range of academic subjects at a German technical university.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Maslach Burnout Inventory Short Form for Students (MBI-SS). Study engagement was evaluated using the Utrecht Work Engagement Scale-Student Survey (UWES-S 9).

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** Sociodemographic data (age, gender), study satisfaction (1-10 scale), academic workload (self-reported weekly hours for classes and preparation), number of semesters studied, and occupational liabilities (hours per week spent on side-jobs).

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None. The study relied exclusively on traditional inferential statistics, specifically Binary Logistic Regression.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** The binary logistic regression models successfully identified significant predictors. For example, higher study satisfaction was protective against exhaustion (OR = 0.702), while increased academic workload slightly elevated the risk for cynicism (OR = 1.013) but also increased study engagement (OR = 1.014).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable (Interpretability relies on standard white-box Odds Ratios [OR] from logistic regression).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The study provides structural recommendations for university administrators, advising the systematic integration of health promotion (nutrition, physical activity, stress management) into the curriculum and fostering a supportive environment to boost study satisfaction, rather than solely focusing on reducing academic workload.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Exceptionally large sample size (N=3,451); expands research beyond the typical focus on medical/nursing students to include all academic disciplines at a technical university; explores the duality of both negative (burnout) and positive (engagement) academic experiences.
* **Key Limitations:** Cross-sectional design prevents drawing definitive causal conclusions; relies entirely on subjective self-assessments (e.g., self-reported workload hours); data is restricted to a single technical university in Germany, limiting generalizability.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Complete absence of objective behavioral data, such as passive digital logging, to verify self-reported study or work hours; absence of predictive Machine Learning (ML) models or Explainable AI (XAI) frameworks; lacks cross-cultural validation, particularly in lower-middle-income countries (LMICs) such as Bangladesh.
