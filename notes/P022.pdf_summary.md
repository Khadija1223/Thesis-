# File Directory: papers/P022.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Muhammad Bimo Wijoyo, Eka Firmansyah, Kenneth Sulthon Alafi Al Hallaj, Irfan Zakariah, Mahbub Junaidi (2026)
* **Publication Venue:** H-Luminia: Education, Psychology and Social Behavior Journal
* **Target Population & Region:** Final year undergraduate students writing their theses | Surabaya, Indonesia
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 215

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To examine the simultaneous and partial impact of academic stress and grit (perseverance) on burnout behavior among final-year students writing their undergraduate theses at an Islamic state university.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Maslach Burnout Inventory Student Survey (MBI-SS) (15 items assessing emotional exhaustion, cynicism, and academic efficacy).

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** Academic Stress (assessed via the Academic Stress Scale measuring workload, fear of failure, interpersonal relationships, time management), Grit (assessed via the Short Grit Scale measuring consistency of interest and perseverance of effort), and Burnout. 

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None. The study utilized traditional statistical analysis, specifically multiple linear regression via IBM SPSS Statistics.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** The multiple linear regression model accounted for 37.7% of the variance in burnout (R² = 0.377). Academic stress was a strong positive predictor (β = 0.512), while grit acted as a significant negative/protective predictor (β = -0.298).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable. (Interpretability relies on standard white-box regression coefficients).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were generated.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The study provides structural recommendations for university counseling centers to implement "dual-track interventions": (1) stress reduction programs focusing on cognitive reframing and time management, and (2) grit enhancement programs incorporating goal visualization and peer mentoring, explicitly rooted in Islamic frameworks of *istiqamah* (consistency) and *mujahadah* (striving).

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Focuses on a highly specific, high-stress academic milestone (thesis writing); effectively targets a unique socio-cultural and religious context (Islamic higher education in Indonesia); successfully integrates the positive psychological construct of 'grit' as a protective buffer against burnout.
* **Key Limitations:** The cross-sectional design restricts the ability to establish true causality between stress, grit, and burnout; relies entirely on a single university population (UINSA), limiting generalizability; 62.3% of the variance in burnout remains unexplained, suggesting missing predictors (e.g., perfectionism, social support).
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Complete absence of objective behavioral data, such as passive digital logging (e.g., tracking typing dynamics or document editing patterns during thesis writing) to complement self-reported stress; absence of predictive Machine Learning (ML) algorithms or Explainable AI (XAI) frameworks to automatically identify at-risk students in real-time.
