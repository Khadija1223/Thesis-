# File Directory: papers/P016.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Jie Zhan, Jiagen Xiang, Yiming Zhang, Xingang Zhao, Ke Zhang, Haining Ou, Lechang Zhan, Aiyun He (2026)
* **Publication Venue:** Frontiers in Psychology
* **Target Population & Region:** Undergraduate students at a Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) university | Guangzhou, China
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 666

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To determine the prevalence and identify the multi-dimensional determinants of academic burnout among undergraduates navigating the unique "dual-curriculum" (traditional and modern medicine) at a TCM university.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Maslach Burnout Inventory-Student Survey (MBI-SS) (Chinese version). The revised Perceived School Climate Scale (PSCS) was also utilized.

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** Demographic factors (age, gender, ethnicity, religious affiliation, pre-university residence), health-related behaviors (diet regularity, daily routine regularity, smoking, alcohol, mental disorder history), academic characteristics (class ranking, grade, major, leadership experience), family background (income, parental education), and perceived school climate (teacher support, student support, autonomy).

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None. The study relied exclusively on traditional statistical methods, including Chi-square tests, Spearman's correlation, and multivariable Binary Logistic Regression via SPSSAU.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** The binary logistic regression model identified five significant independent predictors of academic burnout: religious affiliation (aOR = 5.56), pre-university rural residence (protective, aOR = 0.60), irregular daily routine (aOR = 2.03), lower academic ranking (aOR = 3.87), and perceived teacher support (protective, aOR = 0.78).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable. (Interpretability relies on standard adjusted odds ratios from the regression model).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were generated.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The study proposes four institutional, non-technical strategies: (1) faculty training for supportive mentorship; (2) integrating mandatory time management and sleep hygiene courses; (3) targeted counseling for lower-ranked students and religious minorities; and (4) establishing cross-year peer mentoring initiatives.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Focuses on a highly specific, under-researched population dealing with unique cognitive overload (TCM's dual-curriculum challenge); utilizes a comprehensive multi-tiered ecological framework (combining individual, behavioral, and environmental/school climate factors).
* **Key Limitations:** The cross-sectional design precludes causal inference (e.g., does low teacher support cause burnout, or does burnout skew perception of support?); convenience sampling from a single university limits generalizability; heavy reliance on self-reported data which is susceptible to social desirability bias.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Complete absence of objective behavioral data, such as passive digital logging (e.g., using wearables to track sleep or routine regularity objectively instead of via self-report); lack of predictive Machine Learning (ML) models or Explainable AI (XAI) frameworks; absence of cross-cultural validation in other LMICs (e.g., Bangladesh) or other Complementary and Alternative Medicine (CAM) programs globally.
