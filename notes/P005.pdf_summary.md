# File Directory: papers/P005.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Daniel Cristóbal Andrade-Girón, William Joel Marin-Rodriguez, Marcelo Gumercindo Zuñiga-Rojas, Abrahan Cesar Neri-Ayala, Edgar Tito Susanibar-Ramírez, Miguel Angel Aguilar-Luna-Victoria (2026)
* **Publication Venue:** AI (MDPI)
* **Target Population & Region:** University students | Dharan, Nepal
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 1100

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To develop, compare, and validate multiple ensemble machine learning models for multiclass prediction of student stress, utilizing a rigorous evaluation framework and explainable AI (XAI) analysis.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Public "Student Stress Factors" dataset (Kaggle), which aggregates responses to classify a target variable (`stress_level`) into three ordinal categories: low, medium, and high.

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** 20 predictor variables across psychological, physiological, social, environmental, and academic domains (e.g., sleep quality, blood pressure, social support, self-esteem, depression, anxiety level, living conditions, headache, extracurricular activities, bullying, academic performance, basic needs, noise level).

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** Nine ensemble and boosting algorithms for tabular data: AdaBoost, Gradient Boosting, Random Forest, Extra Trees, Bagging, Voting, Stacking, XGBoost, and LightGBM.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** Gradient Boosting achieved the highest average performance in nested cross-validation (Accuracy: 89.55%, F1-weighted: 89.54%, MCC: 0.845, ROC-AUC weighted: 98.59%). Independent holdout accuracy was 0.8818. (Note: XGBoost and LightGBM showed comparable, statistically equivalent performance).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) for global beeswarm and local waterfall decomposition; LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations) for localized probabilistic prediction analysis.

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations. 
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable system. The findings act as a guide for psychoeducational interventions (e.g., sleep hygiene programs, personalized tutoring, academic support), but the authors emphasize that the model requires prospective validation before integration into institutional early warning systems.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Highly rigorous machine learning pipeline utilizing nested cross-validation, probabilistic calibration, and nonparametric statistical testing (Friedman test); comprehensive multi-criteria comparison of ensemble families; robust integration of both global and local XAI tools (SHAP and LIME).
* **Key Limitations:** Cross-sectional, observational dataset limits causal inference; treatment of ordinal stress levels as nominal classes ignores proximity information between levels; relies entirely on a single public dataset from one university, limiting generalizability.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Although the study targets an LMIC (Nepal), it lacks external validation across other socio-cultural contexts (e.g., Bangladesh) to prove model transferability; relies exclusively on self-reported survey data without multimodal or objective physiological/behavioral sources (e.g., passive digital logging); lacks longitudinal monitoring to track the incidence of stress over time.
