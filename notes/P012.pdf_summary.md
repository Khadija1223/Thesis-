# File Directory: papers/P012.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Yanling Lan, Sihang Liu, Hao Chen (2026)
* **Publication Venue:** Frontiers in Computer Science
* **Target Population & Region:** College students | Fujian Province, China
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 412

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To examine the psychological pathways linking AI affordances (perceived usefulness, inert thinking, perceived enjoyment) to learning burnout, utilizing a serial mediation model based on the I-PACE framework (AI dependence -> AI addiction).
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Learning Burnout (LB) scale (adapted from Schaufeli et al., 2002; essentially derived from the Maslach Burnout Inventory-Student Survey).

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** Likert-scale surveys assessing: Perceived Usefulness of AI, Inert Thinking, Perceived Enjoyment, AI Dependence, AI Addiction, and Learning Burnout. Demographic factors included gender, academic qualification, and major.

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None. The study relied entirely on traditional statistical modeling, specifically Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) executed via SmartPLS and SPSS.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** The PLS-SEM path model successfully validated the serial mediation hypotheses (e.g., perceived usefulness → AI dependence → AI addiction → learning burnout). Model adequacy was verified with SRMR (0.082) and NFI (0.807).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable. (Interpretability relies on standard white-box structural equation path coefficients and mediation analysis).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not a technological intervention system. The study recommends institutional and curricular interventions, including: developing "AI conscious learning modules" to train metacognitive regulation, fostering "Creative Engagement" teaching methods to counter inert thinking, and establishing an "AI Behavior Supervision and Healthy Use System" to track student learning data and provide behavioral feedback.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Innovatively applies the established I-PACE (Interaction of Person-Affect-Cognition-Execution) addiction framework to the modern context of generative AI; clearly defines the mechanistic progression from instrumental AI reliance to pathological AI addiction and subsequent burnout.
* **Key Limitations:** Cross-sectional design prevents definitive causal inference of the sequential pathways; the sample is limited to a single province in China (Fujian), constraining generalizability; reliance on a unidirectional structural model ignores potential cyclical feedback loops between burnout and addiction.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Complete absence of objective behavioral data, such as passive digital logging of actual AI tool usage (e.g., screen time, prompt frequency); lack of longitudinal studies to track the temporal evolution of AI addiction; absence of predictive Machine Learning models or XAI frameworks; lack of cross-cultural validation, particularly in other LMICs like Bangladesh.
