# File Directory: papers/P028.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Hao Zhang, Yanchao Yang, Ying Zhang, Baishuang Qiu, Jiongzhao Yang (2026)
* **Publication Venue:** BMC Psychology
* **Target Population & Region:** University students with programming learning experiences | China (Multiple universities)
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 1,295

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To develop and validate a domain-specific instrument—the Scale of Generative AI Dependency on Programming among University Students—to systematically measure students' cognitive, affective, and behavioral reliance on GenAI tools.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** The newly developed "Scale of Generative AI Dependency on Programming" (based on the ABC model: Affect, Behavior, Cognition). A previously established "Generative Artificial Intelligence Dependency Scale" was used to test criterion-related validity.

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** Self-reported GenAI dependency across three dimensions: Affect (e.g., anxiety/uneasiness when AI is unavailable), Cognition (e.g., belief that AI-generated code is superior to own code), and Behavior (e.g., unconsciously turning to AI even for simple tasks). Demographic variables included gender and birthplace (urban/rural).

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None. The study relied on traditional psychometric scale development techniques, including Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) using SPSS and Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) using the CB-SEM module in SmartPLS.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** The proposed 3-factor CFA model demonstrated a good fit to the data (RMSEA = 0.070, CFI = 0.981, TLI = 0.976), significantly outperforming a unidimensional 1-factor model. The scale also exhibited strong internal consistency (Cronbach’s α = 0.963).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable. (Interpretability relies on standard white-box psychometric factor loadings and goodness-of-fit indices).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The study provides researchers and educators with a validated diagnostic tool to monitor GenAI dependency, aiming to inform future instructional designs and intervention strategies that balance the use of AI with the development of independent computational thinking and coding confidence.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Addresses a highly contemporary educational challenge (GenAI dependence vs. addiction) with a domain-specific tool tailored specifically for programming students; utilizes a large sample size (N=1,295); strongly grounded in the theoretical ABC (Affect, Behavior, Cognition) framework.
* **Key Limitations:** Test-retest reliability was not assessed, leaving the temporal stability of the scale unverified; initial scale items were generated from interviews with instructors rather than the students themselves; convenience sampling via WeChat/QQ groups may limit representativeness.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Complete reliance on self-report questionnaires which are susceptible to social desirability bias (students underreporting their reliance on AI). The authors explicitly call for future studies to integrate objective behavioral data (e.g., IDE behavioral logs, system usage records, learning analytics); absence of predictive Machine Learning (ML) models or Explainable AI (XAI); lacks cross-cultural validation in contexts like Bangladesh (an LMIC).
