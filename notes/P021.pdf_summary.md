# File Directory: papers/P021.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Eldar Yeskuatov, Lee Kien Foo, Sook-Ling Chua (2025)
* **Publication Venue:** Healthcare (MDPI)
* **Target Population & Region:** Undergraduate computing students (IT and Computer Science) | Malaysia
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 688

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To investigate the feasibility of automatically detecting academic burnout symptoms using machine learning models trained *exclusively* on passive university administrative records, eliminating the reliance on psychological surveys.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Maslach Burnout Inventory-General Survey for Students (MBI-GS(S)). (Note: Used strictly to establish the ground-truth target labels for training the ML models, not as input features).

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** The study relies entirely on passively collected institutional administrative records as predictive features. These 18 features include cumulative GPA, total credit hours, academic level, program type, campus location, financial assistance, and admission term.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** None used for prediction. 

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** Logistic Regression (LR), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Naive Bayes (NB), Decision Tree (DT), and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost).
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** Logistic Regression (LR) achieved the highest performance for detecting the Exhaustion dimension, with an F1-score of 68.4% and Recall of 81.2%. Naive Bayes (NB) performed best for Cynicism but with lower metrics (Accuracy = 53.3%, F1-score = 51.3%).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** Feature Importance using the Boruta algorithm to identify and validate statistical associations between administrative features and burnout dimensions. No localized XAI tools (like SHAP/LIME) were used.

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** The study's primary goal is the creation of an actionable early warning system: an automated, unobtrusive burnout detection pipeline that continuously scans university administrative records to flag at-risk students for proactive outreach, bypassing the administrative burden and response biases of traditional surveys.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Highly innovative methodology that completely removes self-report bias from the predictor variables by relying solely on objective administrative data; shifts focus away from medical students to underrepresented computing students; effectively addresses an LMIC/Southeast Asian context (Malaysia).
* **Key Limitations:** The overall predictive performance was modest (average F1 scores ranged from 39.2% for professional efficacy to 64.1% for exhaustion), demonstrating that basic administrative records lack the psychological depth necessary to accurately capture burnout; cross-sectional design.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Although the study utilized passive administrative data, the authors explicitly note the critical missing gap of continuous passive digital/behavioral logging—such as LMS (Learning Management System) interaction logs, attendance tracking, and extracurricular engagement metrics—which are needed to significantly improve model accuracy; lack of advanced Explainable AI (XAI) frameworks to interpret the predictions transparently.
