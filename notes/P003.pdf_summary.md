# File Directory: papers/P003.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Seo-Jun Lee (2026)
* **Publication Venue:** International Journal of Psychology Research
* **Target Population & Region:** Undergraduate university students | Busan, South Korea
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 412

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To examine the relationship between emotional regulation strategies (e.g., cognitive reappraisal, expressive suppression, rumination) and academic burnout, and to assess the mediating role of psychological distress.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Maslach Burnout Inventory-Student Survey (MBI-SS) for burnout; Depression Anxiety Stress Scales (DASS-21) for psychological distress; Emotion Regulation Questionnaire (ERQ) and Cognitive Emotion Regulation Questionnaire (CERQ) for regulation strategies.

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** Emotional regulation strategies (cognitive reappraisal, expressive suppression, acceptance, problem-solving, rumination), academic burnout dimensions (emotional exhaustion, cynicism, academic efficacy), psychological distress (depression, anxiety, stress), and demographic factors (gender, age, year of study).

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None (The study relies entirely on traditional inferential statistics, specifically hierarchical multiple regression and bootstrapped mediation analysis).
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** The final hierarchical regression model explained 35.9% of the total variance in academic burnout scores (ΔR² = 0.312 for emotion regulation strategies specifically).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None/Not Applicable (Uses traditional white-box statistical modeling, such as regression coefficients and mediation pathways).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable system. The paper recommends policy-level interventions such as campus-based emotional regulation training programs (focusing on cognitive reappraisal and problem-solving) and early screening at the start of each semester.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Expands beyond the classic reappraisal-suppression dichotomy by including multiple regulation strategies; uses mediation analysis to explain the mechanism via psychological distress; provides empirical evidence from a collectivist cultural context.
* **Key Limitations:** Cross-sectional design prevents causal interpretation; sample drawn from a single metropolitan university limits generalizability; exclusive reliance on self-report surveys introduces potential shared-method variance bias.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** No integration of behavioral or physiological indicators (specifically lacking passive digital logging); absence of longitudinal or experience-sampling designs to track temporal ordering; lacks representation of other non-Western contexts such as LMICs (e.g., Bangladesh).
