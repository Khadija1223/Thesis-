# File Directory: papers/P025.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Ariel Pizarro-Monjes, David Vicente Rodríguez-Pavez, Alejandro Hugo Pérez-Zúñiga, Marcos Ignacio Rivas-Ojeda, Rocío Glaría-López, Cristhian Pérez-Villalobos, Berta Schulz, Paulina Ortega-Bastidas, Juan Francisco Hernández-Quidel (2026)
* **Publication Venue:** BMC Medical Education
* **Target Population & Region:** Undergraduate health sciences students (Medicine, Physical Therapy, Pharmaceutical Chemistry, Speech Therapy) | Chile
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 879

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To examine the association of academic burnout levels with specific sociodemographic and academic characteristics among health sciences students at a Chilean university.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Maslach Burnout Inventory–Student Survey (MBI-SS), measuring Emotional Exhaustion (EE), Depersonalization (DP), and Lack of Personal Accomplishment (LPA).

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** Sociodemographic and academic characteristics including gender, age, paid employment, course failure, graduating institution type (public, subsidized, private), and year of study.

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None. The study relied on traditional inferential statistics, specifically multiple linear regression models, Mann-Whitney U tests, Kruskal-Wallis H tests, and Spearman’s Rho.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** The multiple linear regression models accounted for modest variances in the burnout subscales: 10.5% for Emotional Exhaustion (EE), 5.8% for Lack of Personal Accomplishment (LPA), and 10.2% for Depersonalization (DP). Notably, being female was a strong predictor of EE, while male gender and paid employment predicted higher DP.
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable. (Interpretability relies on standard white-box regression coefficients).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The study recommends institutional and curricular strategies, such as: (1) strengthening professional exposure and coping tools during pre-clinical years (years 1-3) to prepare students for the clinical transition in year 4; (2) providing targeted support to compensate for educational disadvantages based on high school background; and (3) employing gender-specific psychological interventions, given that men tend to evade (DP) while women experience emotional depletion (EE).

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Large sample size covering a diverse range of health science programs, not just medical students (e.g., Speech Therapy, Pharmaceutical Chemistry); effectively highlights the highly gendered and program-specific manifestations of burnout (EE vs. DP).
* **Key Limitations:** Cross-sectional design prevents the establishment of causality; non-probabilistic quota sampling method and restriction to a single university in Chile limit broad generalizability.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Complete reliance on a self-administered questionnaire without any objective behavioral indicators or passive digital logging; absence of predictive Machine Learning (ML) models and Explainable AI (XAI) to track burnout trajectories; while it provides data from a Latin American context, it lacks cross-regional applicability to other LMICs like Bangladesh.
