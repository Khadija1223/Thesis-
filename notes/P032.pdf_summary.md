# File Directory: papers/P032.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Munassir Alhamami, Abdulaziz Alrabai (2026)
* **Publication Venue:** Humanities & Social Sciences Communications
* **Target Population & Region:** Undergraduate students in English as a Medium of Instruction (EMI) programs | Saudi Arabia
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 497

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To develop and validate a context-specific 17-item burnout scale tailored for students in English as a Medium of Instruction (EMI) programs, addressing the combined academic and language-related stressors they face.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** The newly developed EMI Burnout Scale. It adapts items from the Maslach Burnout Inventory–General Survey for Students (MBI-GS[S]) and the Oldenburg Burnout Inventory (OLBI) to measure Exhaustion, Cynicism, Disengagement, and Academic Efficacy.

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** Self-reported EMI Burnout Scale dimensions (Exhaustion, Cynicism, Disengagement, Academic Efficacy). Demographic factors included gender, academic college/discipline, and year of study.

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None. The study utilized advanced traditional statistical modeling, specifically Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) using a Reflective–Reflective Higher-Order Construct (R-R HOC) approach via SmartPLS.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** The PLS-SEM measurement model demonstrated excellent convergent and discriminant validity (all AVEs > 0.81). The structural model revealed that Exhaustion had the strongest path coefficient (0.955) to overall burnout and the highest predictive relevance (Q²predict = 0.912) and lowest error (RMSE = 0.299).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable. (Interpretability relies on standard white-box PLS-SEM path coefficients, R² values, and Q²predict metrics).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The study recommends structural educational interventions tailored to EMI students: implementing targeted language support (e.g., continuous proficiency workshops), adopting inclusive teaching strategies (e.g., visual aids, glossaries to lower cognitive load), and embedding mental health resources directly into EMI program structures.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Successfully addresses a unique, high-stress subpopulation (students learning complex material in a second language); develops a highly contextualized psychometric tool; utilizes robust structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) to validate the scale.
* **Key Limitations:** The scale was validated exclusively in Saudi Arabia, meaning its stability in other cultural or linguistic settings is untested; the cross-sectional design prevents assessing the temporal development of burnout; the study did not test for measurement invariance across gender or disciplines.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Relies entirely on self-report questionnaires, missing the opportunity to integrate objective behavioral data via passive digital logging (e.g., tracking late-night LMS activity or assignment writing dynamics to corroborate exhaustion); complete absence of predictive Machine Learning (ML) models or Explainable AI (XAI) frameworks; lacks cross-cultural validation in LMICs outside the Middle East, such as Bangladesh.
