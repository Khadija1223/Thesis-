# File Directory: papers/P033.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Balázs Jagodics, Éva Szabó (2022)
* **Publication Venue:** Trends in Psychology
* **Target Population & Region:** Undergraduate university students | Hungary
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 743

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To analyze the structure and reliability of the newly developed University Demand-Resource Questionnaire (UDRQ) and to apply the Job Demand-Resource (JD-R) model to predict student burnout.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Maslach Burnout Inventory-Student Survey (MBI-SS) (measuring emotional exhaustion, cynicism, and reduced accomplishment).

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** Burnout (MBI-SS), University Demands (work style, mental demands, emotional demands, career choice anxiety, conflict with lecturers), University Resources (possibility of personal development, information, feedback, perceived control, support of lecturers), and demographic factors (age, gender, academic year).

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None. The study relied on traditional psychometric and inferential statistics, including Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA), Pearson’s correlation, and Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) via JASP.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** The Structural Equation Model (SEM) demonstrated a good fit to the data (CFI = 0.984, TLI = 0.983, RMSEA = 0.067). The variables included in the model explained a highly significant proportion of the variance in the burnout symptoms: emotional exhaustion ($R^2 = 0.664$), cynicism ($R^2 = 0.721$), and reduced personal accomplishment ($R^2 = 0.636$).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable. (Interpretability relies on standard white-box SEM standardized path coefficients).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The study provides structural recommendations for university administration to balance demands and resources: redesigning training programs to lower mental demands, while simultaneously increasing resources through improved lecturer feedback, better support systems, and ensuring students perceive a clear possibility for personal/career development.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Successfully adapts the prominent occupational Job Demand-Resource (JD-R) theoretical framework into the educational context; validates a novel instrument (UDRQ); utilizes rigorous Structural Equation Modeling to demonstrate that resources can counterbalance demands to mitigate burnout.
* **Key Limitations:** Cross-sectional data collection prevents the analysis of causal links or developmental trajectories over a student's academic career; convenience sampling limits the generalizability of the findings to the broader student population; lacks objective indicators of academic success (e.g., actual GPA or dropout records).
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Complete reliance on subjective self-report questionnaires to measure "work style" and "mental demands," neglecting the potential of objective, passive digital logging (e.g., tracking actual study hours or learning management system engagement); total absence of predictive Machine Learning (ML) models or Explainable AI (XAI) frameworks; geographically limited to Hungary (a European country), leaving gaps regarding LMICs such as Bangladesh.
