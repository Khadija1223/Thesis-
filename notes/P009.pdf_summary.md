# File Directory: papers/P009.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** M. Graça Pereira, Martim Santos, Renata Magalhães, Cláudia Rodrigues, Odete Araújo, Dalila Durães (2025)
* **Publication Venue:** Behavioral Sciences (MDPI)
* **Target Population & Region:** Undergraduate and graduate psychology students | Northern Portugal
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 274

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To analyze the relationship between psychological and lifestyle variables and academic burnout, and to identify distinct burnout risk profiles among psychology students using machine learning algorithms.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Maslach Burnout Inventory—Student Survey (MBI-SS).

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None utilized.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** Psychological well-being (QGBEP), psychological distress (DASS21), difficulties in emotional regulation (DERS), sleep quality (PSQI), physical activity (IPAQ), and dietary inflammatory index (DII) derived from a Food Frequency Questionnaire. 

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** K-means clustering (for initial risk profiling), Random Forest, XGBoost, and Support Vector Machine (SVM) (for supervised classification of the profiles).
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** XGBoost achieved the best overall performance with 97.53% Accuracy, 94.73% F1-score, 90.00% Precision, and 99.99% Recall.
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Black-box. (While K-means was used to establish descriptive cutoffs for profiles, no formal explainable AI techniques like SHAP or LIME were applied to interpret the supervised ML models).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were generated.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The study advocates for a "stepped-care approach" at the institutional level, using the identified variable cutoffs for early screening and implementing psychoeducational programs targeting emotional regulation and healthy lifestyle habits (sleep, anti-inflammatory diet).

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Extends beyond standard psychological variables by integrating detailed lifestyle factors (specifically the dietary inflammatory index); uses a combination of unsupervised (K-means) and supervised ML to establish data-driven risk profiles.
* **Key Limitations:** Cross-sectional design prevents causal conclusions (e.g., potential bidirectional relationship between emotional regulation and burnout); small sample size restricted solely to psychology students from a single Portuguese university limits generalizability.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Complete reliance on self-reported surveys without any objective physiological metrics or passive digital logging (e.g., objective sleep tracking or physical activity logging); lack of longitudinal validation; absence of XAI methods to transparently interpret the ML models; lack of validation in non-Western or LMIC settings (e.g., Bangladesh).
