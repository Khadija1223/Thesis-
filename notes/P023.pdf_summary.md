# File Directory: papers/P023.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Santiago Cotobal Rodeles, Francisco Javier Martín Sánchez, Manuel Martínez-Sellés (2025)
* **Publication Venue:** Journal of Clinical Medicine
* **Target Population & Region:** Physicians and Medical Students | Global (Narrative Literature Review)
* **Sample Size ($N$):** Not applicable (Literature Review)

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To comprehensively review the literature on physician and medical student burnout across specialties, assess its consequences on mental health and patient safety, and propose evidence-based strategies for prevention and treatment.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Discusses various tools utilized across the literature, predominantly highlighting the Maslach Burnout Inventory (MBI-HSS, MBI-SS, and abbreviated versions), the Stanford Physician Wellness survey, and the Mini-Z Work-Life instrument.

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** The review explicitly notes a growing trend/opportunity in using passive data: "wearables and biosensors can also be used to detect burnout-related symptoms, detecting, for instance, low step count, time in bed, heart rate, and heart rate variability." It also highlights Electronic Health Record (EHR) workload ("technostress") as a major systemic indicator/cause of burnout.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** Synthesizes indicators across studies including: academic pressure, workload/time constraints, emotional strain, lack of support systems, financial pressures, sleep disruption, demographic factors (age, sex, ethnicity), and clinical specialty.

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None directly evaluated (Narrative Review). However, the paper identifies integrating Artificial Intelligence (AI) as a crucial future direction to reduce administrative burdens (e.g., via digital scribes).
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** None / Not Applicable.
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable.

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic recommendations provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The review outlines a comprehensive, multi-level plan including: Institutional/Systemic changes (workload management, AI-assisted documentation tools to reduce EHR burden, fair shift scheduling) and Individual-level support (confidential counseling, resilience training, sleep hygiene promotion, peer mentorship).

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Provides a holistic, highly structured overview of the entire burnout ecosystem for medical professionals (from etiology to financial costs to interventions); clearly maps out the dual burden of clinical and academic pressures on medical students.
* **Key Limitations:** As a narrative review rather than a systematic review or meta-analysis, the selected literature may be subject to author selection bias.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** The review explicitly identifies several gaps: lack of longitudinal studies to track burnout progression; scarce data on how burnout affects specific minority ethnicities and sexual/gender minorities; the need for clinical trials assessing the efficacy of organizational-level wellness interventions. It also notes that while most studies are Western-centric, burnout in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) appears to be just as high or higher, representing a clear geographical gap.
