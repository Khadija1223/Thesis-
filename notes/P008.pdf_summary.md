# File Directory: papers/P008.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Liang Zhe Chong, Lee Kien Foo, Sook-Ling Chua (2025)
* **Publication Venue:** Behavioral Sciences (MDPI)
* **Target Population & Region:** Systematic review covering various student populations (Primary, Secondary/High School, and University students) | Global (Multiple countries reviewed)
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 38 studies included in the systematic review.

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To systematically review recent literature (2019–2024) investigating the diverse factors contributing to burnout across different student populations and to summarize mitigation strategies.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** The review found that the Maslach Burnout Inventory (MBI) was the most widely used tool (18 papers), followed by the School Burnout Inventory (SBI) (6 papers). Other tools included the Copenhagen Burnout Inventory, Burnout Assessment Tool, and Oldenburg Burnout Inventory.

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None utilized directly (this is a literature review). However, the review identifies "problematic internet use" and "excessive use of digital teaching technologies" as key risk factors, which were uniformly measured via self-reports in the underlying studies rather than passive logging.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** The review synthesizes predictors into three categories: Environmental Factors (school environment, impact of digital technologies, social/family support), Psychosocial Factors (psychosocial competencies like grit and emotional regulation, coping strategies), and Other Factors (academic performance, demographics, lifestyle choices like sleep and exercise).

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None. The review notes that the field overwhelmingly relies on traditional statistical methods, primarily Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) (13 papers), Linear Regression (10 papers), Logistic Regression (5 papers), and Latent Profile Analysis (4 papers).
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** Not applicable (Systematic Review of statistical studies).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable.

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic recommendations provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The review recommends policy-level interventions: fostering supportive school environments, teaching healthy internet habits to mitigate digital isolation, building psychosocial competencies (e.g., emotional regulation), and promoting adaptive coping strategies.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Provides a highly comprehensive global synthesis of risk and protective factors for student burnout across all educational levels; clearly categorizes variables into environmental, psychosocial, and demographic domains.
* **Key Limitations:** Limited to articles published between 2019 and 2024; the review's findings are constrained by the methodologies of the primary studies, the vast majority of which are cross-sectional rather than longitudinal.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** The review starkly highlights a methodological gap across the field: the complete reliance on self-report questionnaires (like the MBI) rather than objective behavioral data such as passive digital logging. Furthermore, the absence of predictive Machine Learning (ML) models and explainable AI (XAI) frameworks in the reviewed literature points to a significant gap in creating proactive, technology-driven early warning systems for students.
