# File Directory: papers/P024.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Caterina Fiorilli, Daniela Barni, Claudia Russo, Vanessa Marchetti, Giacomo Angelini, Luciano Romano (2022)
* **Publication Venue:** International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health (MDPI)
* **Target Population & Region:** University students (both working and non-working) | Rome/Bergamo, Italy
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 494

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To investigate differences in the academic burnout experience among university students based on their gender and worker status (employed vs. non-employed students).
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Burnout Assessment Tool–Core Symptoms short version (BAT-C-Short version) (12 items measuring exhaustion, mental distance, cognitive impairment, and emotional impairment).

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** Burnout symptoms (BAT-C), sociodemographic information (age, gender, employment status), and academic factors (academic curricula/degree type, year of enrollment).

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None. The study utilized traditional psychometric and inferential statistics: Measurement Invariance testing (using Mplus), Multivariate Analysis of Variance (MANOVA), and Multivariate Analysis of Covariance (MANCOVA) (using SPSS/Jamovi).
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** The MANOVA demonstrated significant gender differences (Wilks' Lambda = 0.847, p < 0.01). Female students showed significantly higher levels of exhaustion, cognitive impairment, and emotional impairment than male students. Interestingly, no significant differences were found based on worker status or the interaction between gender and worker status.
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable. (Interpretability relies on standard white-box MANOVA/MANCOVA test statistics).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The study recommends that academic staff and policymakers consider gender as a fundamental factor when designing institutional prevention programs to reduce burnout and subsequent academic dropout.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Validates the newer, highly comprehensive Burnout Assessment Tool (BAT-C) for the university student population; rigorously tests for measurement invariance across gender and worker status before running mean comparisons.
* **Key Limitations:** Cross-sectional design fails to capture the developmental trajectory of burnout throughout a student's academic career; the study lacked detailed variables regarding the working students' employment (e.g., number of hours worked per week, type of work), limiting the depth of the worker-status findings.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Total reliance on a single self-report instrument (BAT-C) without any objective behavioral or physiological data (such as passive digital logging); complete absence of predictive Machine Learning (ML) algorithms or Explainable AI (XAI); geographically limited to Italy (a high-income European country), leaving gaps regarding LMICs (e.g., Bangladesh).
