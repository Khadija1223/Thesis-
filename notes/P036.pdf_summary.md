# File Directory: papers/P036.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Neva Vidović, Maja Vidović, Ana Havelka Meštrović (2026)
* **Publication Venue:** Sustainability, Organization, Business and Economic Research (SOBER) / CIBES 2025 Conference
* **Target Population & Region:** Undergraduate students enrolled in an international business program | Croatia
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 223

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To explore the prevalence and profile of burnout and anxiety among university students navigating the specific stressors of an international, cross-cultural academic environment.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Maslach Burnout Inventory (MBI) (utilizing the 22-item occupational version) and the Anxiety Screening Questionnaire (ASQ).

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** Self-reported burnout dimensions (emotional exhaustion, depersonalization, reduced personal achievement), anxiety levels, and demographic data limited to gender.

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None. The study relied exclusively on traditional inferential and descriptive statistics, specifically independent samples t-tests and Pearson correlations using IBM SPSS.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** The statistical analysis revealed a high prevalence of moderate to severe burnout (55.5%) and anxiety (52.6%). Burnout and anxiety were strongly correlated (r = 0.609, p < .001). Female students reported significantly higher emotional exhaustion than male students (p = .001, Cohen's d = 0.55).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable. (Interpretability relies on standard white-box statistical tests).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were generated.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The study provides structural recommendations for academic institutions: rebuilding academic confidence through constructive feedback and mentoring programs; prioritizing accessible mental health services (counseling and therapy); and actively encouraging healthy social connections (e.g., student clubs) to combat the isolation frequently experienced by international students.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Focuses on a unique subpopulation (international students studying abroad) facing distinct cross-cultural adaptation stressors; explicitly differentiates between the constructs of chronic burnout and general anxiety.
* **Key Limitations:** Cross-sectional design prevents the establishment of causal relationships; the sample size is relatively small and drawn from a single college in Croatia, limiting broad generalizability; the study utilized the occupational MBI rather than the student-specific MBI-SS, which the authors justify but remains a methodological limitation.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Complete reliance on subjective self-report questionnaires, which the authors acknowledge could lead to inflated perceptions of burnout. There is a total absence of objective behavioral data or passive digital logging to corroborate these high stress levels; lack of predictive Machine Learning (ML) models or Explainable AI (XAI) frameworks; geographically centered in an upper-middle-income EU country (Croatia), leaving gaps regarding LMICs such as Bangladesh.
