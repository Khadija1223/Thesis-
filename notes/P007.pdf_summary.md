# File Directory: papers/P007.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Willian Sierra Barón, Andrés Gómez Acosta, Laura Natalia Calceto Garavito, Hernán David Romero Reyes, Juan David Salazar Salamanca (2025)
* **Publication Venue:** Revista Informes Psicológicos
* **Target Population & Region:** Undergraduate psychology university students | Colombia (Andean and Amazonian regions)
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 282

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To determine the psychometric properties (validity and reliability) of the Maslach Burnout Inventory-Student Survey (MBI-SS) in university students from southern Colombia, an area characterized by rurality, socioeconomic precariousness, and armed conflict.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Maslach Burnout Inventory-Student Survey (MBI-SS). The Utrecht Work Engagement Scale-Student Version (UWES-S) was also utilized to assess concurrent and discriminant validity.

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** Sociodemographic questionnaire (gender, faculty, period of study, socioeconomic level, marital status), MBI-SS dimensions (exhaustion, cynicism, academic efficacy), and UWES-S dimensions (vigor, dedication, absorption).

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** None. The study strictly employs traditional psychometric validation methods, specifically exploratory factor analysis and confirmatory factor analysis via Structural Equation Modeling (SEM).
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** A 14-item structural equation model (excluding item 15) demonstrated the best fit, explaining 74.76% of the cumulative variance with adequate goodness-of-fit indicators (CFI > .90, RMSEA < .100) and high internal consistency (McDonald's ω = .83).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** None / Not Applicable (Relies on traditional white-box psychometric statistics and structural equation modeling).

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations are provided.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. The study recommends using the validated MBI-SS tool to systematically monitor burnout, identify risk profiles in public institutions, and guide the implementation of curricular strategies, tutoring, and mental health services tailored to specific territorial/regional conditions.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Expands burnout research to a historically under-researched, vulnerable region in Latin America (southern Colombia); utilizes robust psychometric validation techniques including gender invariance testing; highlights the impact of socio-educational contexts on burnout.
* **Key Limitations:** Cross-sectional design; purposive/convenience sampling; restricted to undergraduate psychology students, which limits generalizability to other academic disciplines or populations.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Complete reliance on traditional self-reported surveys with no integration of objective, passive digital logging or behavioral markers; absence of predictive machine learning frameworks to anticipate burnout before it occurs; need for broader longitudinal studies in diverse LMIC contexts outside of Latin America (e.g., Bangladesh).
