# File Directory: papers/P010.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Hanif Abdul Rahman, Madeline Kwicklis, Mohammad Ottom, Areekul Amornsriwatanakul, Khadizah H. Abdul-Mumin, Michael Rosenberg, Ivo D. Dinov (2023)
* **Publication Venue:** Bioengineering (MDPI)
* **Target Population & Region:** University students | Southeast Asia (ASEAN countries: Brunei Darussalam, Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, and Vietnam)
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 15,366

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To predict and classify negative mental well-being among university students using machine learning models based on self-reported health-risk behaviors and demographic indicators.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** A multi-site online cross-sectional survey categorizing mental well-being into a binary outcome (positive or negative/poor) alongside non-communicable disease risk factors.

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used.
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** 20 features including health-risk behaviors (number of sports activities per week, sedentary hours, hours of sleep, fruit/vegetable intake, salt intake, eating snacks/fast food, sugar beverages, alcohol consumption, smoking) and demographic/academic variables (body mass index [BMI], age, gender, grade point average [GPA], living arrangement).

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** Generalized Linear Model (Logistic Regression), k-Nearest Neighbor (kNN), Naïve Bayes, Neural Networks, Random Forest, Recursive Partitioning (RPART), Bagging, and Adaptive Boosting.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** Random Forest achieved the highest overall performance with an Accuracy of 0.901, Kappa of 0.801, Sensitivity of 0.980, Specificity of 0.815, and AUC of 0.966.
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** Global Feature Importance (Variable importance was evaluated using Mean Decrease in Accuracy and Mean Decrease in Gini Index). No localized XAI tools like SHAP or LIME were used.

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations were provided directly to students.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable technological system. However, the study proposes policy-level system features: establishing a central data analytics hub for the ASEAN University Network (AUN), utilizing mHealth apps to rapidly collect the top 5 salient features (BMI, sports, GPA, sedentary hours, age) to shorten survey times, and creating a digital dashboard for active monitoring by university stakeholders.

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Exceptionally large, multi-national sample size representing the Southeast Asian (ASEAN) region; robust comparison of multiple machine learning classifiers; successfully identifies a concise, practical set of easily measurable behavioral features to predict mental well-being.
* **Key Limitations:** Cross-sectional design prevents causal inferences; relies entirely on self-reported survey data, making it vulnerable to reporting bias; binary classification of mental well-being may oversimplify complex psychological states.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Total absence of objective, passive digital logging or continuous physiological data (the authors explicitly suggest future models incorporate biofeedback such as EEG, ECG, and heart rate); lack of individualized explainable AI (XAI) techniques to provide transparent local predictions; need for longitudinal data to track mental health trajectories.
