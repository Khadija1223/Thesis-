# File Directory: papers/P002.pdf

## 1. Metadata
* **Authors & Year:** Shuo Gong, Yunjing Li, Haijiang Li (2026)
* **Publication Venue:** Acta Psychologica
* **Target Population & Region:** Junior and senior high school students (Adolescents) | China (Five cities: Ningbo, Xinji, Zhengzhou, Hengyang, and Jianli)
* **Sample Size ($N$):** 4870

## 2. Core Focus & Psychological Metrics
* **Primary Objective:** To examine the multi-level ecological structure of academic burnout in adolescents by combining machine learning with network analysis, identifying key multi-ecological predictors and their structural interrelations.
* **Burnout / Mental Health Assessment Tool:** Student Burnout Inventory (SBI) (adapted for Chinese adolescents, based on the Maslach Burnout Inventory–Student Survey). Depressive symptoms were measured using PHQ-9.

## 3. Indicators & Feature Sets
* **Digital Wellbeing / Passive Indicators:** None used. (Digital stress measures such as social media online vigilance and smartphone withdrawal were captured entirely via self-report).
* **Self-Reported / Survey Indicators:** 61 variables spanning individual, family, school, and digital domains. Key indicators include academic burnout, depressive symptoms (PHQ9), psychological resilience (PsyRes), subjective happiness, physical aggressive behavior, teacher-student relationships (TSRe), school belonging, academic pressure, parent-child alienation, and digital stress (social media online vigilance, smartphone withdrawal).

## 4. Machine Learning & Explainability (XAI)
* **ML Algorithms Evaluated:** Logistic Regression, Naive Bayes, Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest, AdaBoost, XGBoost, and LightGBM.
* **Best Performing Model & Metrics:** Support Vector Machine (SVM) with an RBF kernel achieved the best overall performance (AUC = 0.830, F1-Score = 0.750, Accuracy = 0.747).
* **XAI / Interpretability Techniques Used:** Kernel SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) was used to extract global feature importance and directional effects. RFECV (Recursive Feature Elimination with Cross-Validation) was used for feature selection, followed by Gaussian Graphical Model (GGM) network analysis to interpret the structural relationships between the selected features.

## 5. Recommendations & Interventions
* **Personalized Recommendations Provided?** No algorithmic personalized recommendations are generated for users directly. The study offers population-level recommendations for school policy and resource allocation.
* **Actionable System Features:** Not an actionable system. Recommends implementing a multi-tiered, school-based support framework (e.g., targeted counseling for co-occurring emotional/digital difficulties, digital literacy education, and boundary-setting guidance for those with high online vigilance).

## 6. Critical Evaluation & Research Gaps
* **Main Strengths:** Integration of predictive machine learning (SVM) with structural network analysis (GGM) to understand both risk factors and their interconnections; large and geographically diverse sample within China; consideration of multi-ecological factors including the digital domain.
* **Key Limitations:** Cross-sectional design prevents causal inferences; reliance on an abbreviated set of items for the burnout measure rather than the full scale; reliance solely on self-reported data subject to reporting bias.
* **Unaddressed Research Gaps:** Lack of multi-source and objective behavioral indicators, specifically the absence of passive digital logging to measure true digital behavior; absence of longitudinal studies to capture dynamic risk evolution; need for cross-cultural validation in other settings (e.g., other LMICs like Bangladesh, as the study focuses strictly on China).
